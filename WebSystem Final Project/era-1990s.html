◆ The Connected World ◆

The 1990s

The World Wide Web shrank the planet. Email replaced letters. Search engines organized human knowledge. The dot-com boom brought millions online — and crashed just as fast. The age of connection had begun.

◆ Overview ◆

The Internet Age

The World Goes Online

The 1990s was defined by one invention above all others — the World Wide Web. Tim Berners-Lee's creation turned the Internet from a tool used by scientists and governments into a public network that anyone could access with a home computer and a phone line.

By the mid-1990s, browsers like Netscape Navigator made the Web accessible to ordinary users. Companies rushed to create websites, email became a standard form of communication, and the dot-com bubble inflated as investors poured money into any business with ".com" in its name.

✦

World Wide Web (1991) — Tim Berners-Lee makes the Web publicly available. Within 5 years, millions of websites exist.

✦

Windows 95 (1995) — Microsoft's landmark OS introduced the Start button, taskbar, and plug-and-play hardware support.

✦

Google (1998) — Founded in a Stanford dorm room, Google's PageRank algorithm changed how we find information.

✦

DVD (1995) — Digital Versatile Disc replaced VHS tapes for video and offered 4.7GB of storage per disc.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

1991
World Wide Web Goes Public
Tim Berners-Lee makes the Web publicly available. The first website is info.cern.ch — still accessible today.
1993
Mosaic Browser
The Mosaic browser makes the Web graphical, displaying images alongside text. Web usage grows 341,634% in one year.
1994
Netscape Navigator and Amazon
Netscape Navigator becomes the dominant web browser. Jeff Bezos founds Amazon as an online bookstore.
1995
Windows 95, eBay, and JavaScript
Microsoft's Windows 95 launches to massive fanfare. eBay and JavaScript are both created in the same year.
1996
First Smartphones
Nokia releases early mobile phones with basic computing features. Palm Pilot popularizes the PDA (personal digital assistant).
1997
Deep Blue Defeats Kasparov
IBM's Deep Blue chess computer defeats world champion Garry Kasparov — the first time a computer beats the world's best human player.
1998
Google Founded
Larry Page and Sergey Brin found Google. iMac G3 launches with colorful translucent designs, making computers a fashion statement.
1999
Wi-Fi Standard (802.11b)
The IEEE 802.11b Wi-Fi standard is released, enabling wireless Internet — the beginning of the wireless world.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 1990s

The Web Changes Everything

World Wide Web
A system of hyperlinked documents accessible via the Internet. Invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN and made publicly available in 1991.
Email Goes Mainstream
Electronic mail becomes a standard communication tool. Hotmail (1996) offered free webmail to anyone with an internet connection.
DVD
Digital Versatile Disc offered 4.7GB of storage — enough for a full movie in digital quality. Replaced VHS as the home video standard.
Wi-Fi
The 802.11b wireless standard (1999) allowed computers to connect to the Internet without cables — starting the wireless revolution.
