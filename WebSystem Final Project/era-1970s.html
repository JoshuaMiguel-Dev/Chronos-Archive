◆ The Birth of Personal Computing ◆

The 1970s

The microprocessor put an entire computer on a single chip. What once required a room and a team of engineers could now sit on a desk. The dream of a personal computer was becoming real.

◆ Overview ◆

The Microprocessor Revolution

A Computer on a Chip

The defining invention of the 1970s was the microprocessor — a single chip containing all the core components of a computer's central processing unit. Intel's 4004, released in 1971, was the first commercially available microprocessor. It had 2,300 transistors and ran at 740 kHz.

This single invention sparked the personal computer revolution. Companies like Apple, Commodore, and Radio Shack began selling computers directly to the public. By the end of the decade, computing was no longer limited to governments and corporations.

✦

Intel 4004 (1971) — The world's first commercial microprocessor. 2,300 transistors on a chip smaller than a thumbnail.

✦

Altair 8800 (1975) — A kit computer that sparked the hobbyist computing movement. Bill Gates and Paul Allen wrote software for it — founding Microsoft.

✦

Apple II (1977) — One of the first mass-produced personal computers, with color graphics and a keyboard.

✦

Floppy Disk (1971) — IBM introduced the 8-inch floppy disk as a portable storage medium — a revolution for sharing data.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

1971
Intel 4004 — First Microprocessor
Intel releases the 4004, the world's first single-chip microprocessor. It costs $200 and performs 60,000 operations per second.
1971
Floppy Disk Introduced
IBM introduces the 8-inch floppy disk, giving users a way to physically carry and share programs and data.
1972
C Programming Language
Dennis Ritchie at Bell Labs creates the C programming language, which becomes the foundation of most modern operating systems.
1973
Ethernet Invented
Robert Metcalfe at Xerox PARC invents Ethernet — the technology that connects computers in local networks, still used today.
1975
Altair 8800 and Microsoft
The Altair 8800 kit computer launches. Bill Gates and Paul Allen write BASIC for it and found Microsoft shortly after.
1976
Apple Founded
Steve Jobs, Steve Wozniak, and Ronald Wayne found Apple Computer. The Apple I, hand-built by Wozniak, sells for $666.66.
1977
The Trinity of Personal Computers
Apple II, TRS-80, and Commodore PET are released in the same year — the first mass-market personal computers.
1979
VisiCalc — First Spreadsheet
VisiCalc becomes the first spreadsheet program, giving businesses a compelling reason to buy personal computers.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 1970s

The Tools That Brought Computing Home

Microprocessor
An entire CPU on a single chip. The Intel 4004 started it, the 8080 and 6502 popularized it in home computers.
Floppy Disk
Portable magnetic storage that let users carry programs between computers. Came in 8-inch, 5.25-inch, and later 3.5-inch sizes.
Personal Computer Kits
Computers sold as kits for hobbyists to assemble. The Altair 8800 was the most famous, inspiring a generation of inventors.
Ethernet
A standard for connecting computers in a local area network (LAN). Invented at Xerox PARC and still the backbone of office networks today.
