◆ The GUI Revolution ◆

The 1980s

Computers left the garage and entered the living room. The graphical user interface replaced cryptic commands with icons and windows. Software became an industry. Gaming sparked a cultural revolution.

◆ Overview ◆

The Desktop Era

Computers for Everyone

The 1980s transformed computing from a hobbyist passion into an everyday household item. IBM's entry into personal computing in 1981 legitimized the market, while Apple's Macintosh in 1984 showed the world that computers could be intuitive and beautiful.

Microsoft's MS-DOS became the dominant operating system for IBM-compatible PCs, and by the end of the decade, Windows was establishing the graphical interface standard that would define computing for the next three decades.

✦

IBM PC (1981) — IBM's open architecture allowed other manufacturers to build compatible machines, creating the PC industry as we know it.

✦

Apple Macintosh (1984) — The first mass-market computer with a mouse-driven graphical user interface, introduced by the famous "1984" Super Bowl ad.

✦

MS-DOS (1981) — Microsoft's disk operating system came bundled with IBM PCs, making Microsoft the dominant software company.

✦

CD-ROM (1985) — Brought high-capacity read-only storage to personal computers, enabling multimedia software and encyclopedias.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

1981
IBM Personal Computer
IBM releases the IBM PC with an Intel 8088 processor and MS-DOS. Its open architecture sparks a massive industry of "IBM compatible" clones.
1982
Commodore 64
The Commodore 64 becomes the best-selling personal computer model of all time, with over 17 million units sold.
1983
Internet Protocol (TCP/IP)
ARPANET adopts TCP/IP as its standard, creating the foundation of what will become the modern Internet.
1984
Apple Macintosh
Apple introduces the Macintosh with a mouse, graphical icons, and windows. It sells 70,000 units in the first 100 days.
1985
Microsoft Windows 1.0
Microsoft releases Windows 1.0, a graphical extension to MS-DOS. Though slow, it establishes the Windows brand.
1985
CD-ROM Drives
CD-ROM drives begin appearing in personal computers, offering 650MB of storage on a single disc.
1987
IBM PS/2 and VGA Graphics
IBM introduces VGA (Video Graphics Array), the graphics standard that enables 256 colors on screen at once.
1989
Tim Berners-Lee Proposes the Web
Working at CERN, Tim Berners-Lee proposes the World Wide Web — a system of hyperlinked documents accessible over the Internet.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 1980s

The Desktop Revolution

The GUI
Graphical User Interface — icons, windows, and a mouse replaced text commands, making computers accessible to non-technical users.
CD-ROM
Compact Disc Read-Only Memory brought massive storage capacity to personal computers, enabling encyclopedias and multimedia software.
Video Games
The NES (1985) revived the game industry after the 1983 crash. Home gaming became a mainstream entertainment form.
Laser Printer
The HP LaserJet (1984) brought professional-quality printing to offices, combining with desktop publishing software.
