◆ The Space Age ◆

The 1960s

The decade where computing reached for the stars — literally. Integrated circuits shrank computers, time-sharing allowed multiple users, and NASA used computers to send humans to the Moon.

◆ Overview ◆

The Turning Point

Smaller, Smarter, Faster

The 1960s was a decade of transformation. The invention of the integrated circuit meant dozens of transistors could be packed onto a single chip, replacing the bulky and unreliable vacuum tubes of the previous decade.

Computers began to shrink from room-sized behemoths into smaller "minicomputers." Universities started using them for research, businesses adopted them for record-keeping, and governments relied on them for everything from census data to missile guidance.

✦

Apollo Guidance Computer (1966) — Guided astronauts to the Moon with just 4KB of RAM and 72KB of storage. A modern USB drive holds billions of times more data.

✦

IBM System/360 (1964) — A family of computers that could run the same software regardless of size — the first scalable computer architecture.

✦

Time-Sharing — Multiple users could now share a single computer simultaneously, each getting a "slice" of processing time.

✦

ARPANET (1969) — The US military's experimental network that would eventually become the Internet.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

1960
COBOL Released
COBOL becomes available for business computing, allowing non-scientists to write programs for the first time.
1961
First Time-Sharing System
MIT's Compatible Time-Sharing System (CTSS) allows multiple users to use a computer at the same time.
1963
ASCII Standard Created
The American Standard Code for Information Interchange is established — a standard way to represent text in computers still used today.
1964
IBM System/360 Launches
IBM introduces a family of computers with a unified architecture, revolutionizing how businesses used computers.
1965
Moore's Law Proposed
Gordon Moore predicts that the number of transistors on a chip will double roughly every two years — a prediction that held true for decades.
1966
Apollo Guidance Computer
NASA deploys the AGC aboard Apollo spacecraft — one of the first computers to use integrated circuits in a critical real-world mission.
1968
The Mother of All Demos
Doug Engelbart demonstrates the computer mouse, video conferencing, word processing, and hypertext — 50 years ahead of its time.
1969
ARPANET Goes Live
The first message is sent over ARPANET — a network connecting four universities. The Internet is born.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 1960s

The Tools That Changed Everything

Integrated Circuit
Multiple transistors on a single silicon chip. Replaced vacuum tubes and made computers smaller, faster, and more reliable.
The Computer Mouse
Invented by Doug Engelbart in 1964. The first pointing device that allowed users to interact with computers visually.
ARPANET
The first packet-switched network, connecting universities and government research centers. The direct ancestor of the Internet.
Minicomputers
Smaller computers like the PDP-8 (1965) brought computing to universities and smaller businesses for the first time.
