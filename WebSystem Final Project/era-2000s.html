◆ The Digital Millennium ◆

The 2000s

Social media connected billions. Smartphones put the Internet in everyone's pocket. YouTube democratized video. Facebook redefined friendship. The cloud moved computing off the desktop and into the sky.

◆ Overview ◆

The Social Web

Computing Becomes Personal Again

After the dot-com crash of 2000–2001, the Internet reinvented itself as "Web 2.0" — a participatory web where users created the content. Blogs replaced newspaper columns, Wikipedia replaced encyclopedias, and YouTube allowed anyone to broadcast to the world.

The decade's most transformative moment came in 2007 when Steve Jobs unveiled the iPhone — a touchscreen computer that fit in your pocket. Within years, smartphones changed how billions of people communicated, consumed media, navigated cities, and viewed the world.

✦

iPod (2001) — "1,000 songs in your pocket." Apple's music player changed how we consume music and paved the way for the iPhone.

✦

Facebook (2004) — Founded by Mark Zuckerberg in his Harvard dorm. By 2009 it had 300 million users.

✦

iPhone (2007) — Apple's revolutionary smartphone combined phone, music player, and Internet device into one touchscreen device.

✦

AWS (2006) — Amazon Web Services launched cloud computing, allowing businesses to rent computing power instead of buying servers.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

2001
iPod and iTunes
Apple launches the iPod and iTunes, changing the music industry forever. Legal digital music downloads become mainstream.
2001
Wikipedia Launched
Jimmy Wales and Larry Sanger launch Wikipedia — a free encyclopedia anyone can edit. It grows to millions of articles in hundreds of languages.
2003
Skype and MySpace
Skype brings free voice and video calling over the Internet. MySpace becomes the first major social network.
2004
Facebook Launches
Mark Zuckerberg launches Facebook from his Harvard dorm room. It opens to the public in 2006.
2005
YouTube Founded
Three former PayPal employees found YouTube. Google acquires it the following year for $1.65 billion.
2006
Amazon AWS and Twitter
Amazon launches AWS, beginning the cloud computing era. Twitter launches as a microblogging platform limited to 140 characters.
2007
The iPhone
Steve Jobs introduces the iPhone — "an iPod, a phone, and an Internet communicator." It revolutionizes mobile computing.
2008
Android and the App Store
Google launches Android. Apple opens the App Store, creating a new software economy worth billions.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 2000s

The Social and Mobile Revolution

The Smartphone
The iPhone merged phone, music player, and web browser. Combined with the App Store, it created an entirely new software economy.
Cloud Computing
AWS and other cloud platforms allowed businesses and developers to rent computing power on-demand, without buying physical servers.
Social Media
Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube transformed the Internet from a content-consumption platform into a social space where everyone could publish.
Bluetooth
Bluetooth became standard in phones and laptops, enabling wireless headphones, keyboards, and device pairing without cables.
