◆ The Era of Giants ◆

The 1950s

The decade when computing was born — massive machines made of vacuum tubes that filled entire rooms, consuming power like small cities, yet capable of performing calculations that would take humans years to complete.

◆ Overview ◆

The Beginning

The Age of Titans

The 1950s marked the true dawn of the computer age. These early machines were built using vacuum tubes — glass tubes that controlled electrical signals — and were so large they required entire buildings to house them. Operating one required teams of trained engineers and mathematicians.

Despite their enormous size and cost, these computers were revolutionary. For the first time in history, machines could process complex mathematical calculations at speeds no human could match. Governments and universities rushed to build and acquire these powerful tools.

✦

ENIAC (1945–1955) — One of the first general-purpose computers. It weighed 30 tons, used 18,000 vacuum tubes, and consumed 150 kilowatts of power.

✦

IBM 701 (1952) — IBM's first commercial scientific computer. Rented at $15,000/month — more than most annual salaries at the time.

✦

UNIVAC I (1951) — The first commercial computer in the US, famously used to predict the 1952 presidential election results.

✦

Storage — Memory was measured in kilobytes. Magnetic drum memory was the primary form of storage.

◆ Timeline ◆

Key Events

Milestones of the Decade

1950
Alan Turing's Imitation Game
Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," proposing what became known as the Turing Test — a benchmark for machine intelligence.
1951
UNIVAC I — First Commercial Computer
Remington Rand delivers the UNIVAC I to the US Census Bureau, marking the start of commercial computing in America.
1952
IBM Enters the Market
IBM releases the IBM 701, its first commercial scientific computer, beginning IBM's dominance of the computing industry.
1953
Magnetic Core Memory
MIT's Whirlwind computer uses magnetic core memory — a huge improvement in reliability and speed over previous storage methods.
1954
FORTRAN Begins Development
IBM begins developing FORTRAN (Formula Translation), the first high-level programming language, released in 1957.
1956
First Hard Disk Drive
IBM introduces the RAMAC 305, the world's first computer with a hard disk drive. It stored 5MB and was the size of two refrigerators.
1958
The Integrated Circuit
Jack Kilby at Texas Instruments invents the integrated circuit — a breakthrough that would eventually eliminate vacuum tubes forever.
1959
COBOL Language Proposed
Grace Hopper and colleagues develop COBOL, a programming language designed for business data processing still used in banking today.
◆ Key Inventions ◆

Technology of the 1950s

What Made It Possible

Vacuum Tubes
Glass tubes that acted as electronic switches. Thousands were needed per computer, generated enormous heat, and failed frequently.
Magnetic Drum Memory
A rotating metal cylinder coated with magnetic material used to store data. It was the primary memory technology of the era.
Punch Cards
Paper cards with holes punched in specific positions were used to input data and programs into early computers.
Teletype Machines
Used as output devices, teletype machines printed results from computations onto paper — the first "monitors."
